# Prioritize the Most Valuable Space

Pick the most valuable area, decision or ownership you can credibly take over
1–2 years, and make sure the next step runs. The first step can be small;
the target needn't be.

## Method

0. If `<AT memory root>/knowledge/general/user-profile.md`
   ([root](../README.md#at-memory-root)) has `## Priority profile`, apply its
   traps as guards. Never copy it anywhere.
1. Start from the user's input. If it's thin, check the roadmap, tickets and
   knowledge base. Mark unknowns `?`; never invent facts.
2. For each decider or affected person, work out what they would prefer most.
   Cite what they said or wrote, and mark the rest as inferred.
3. Find product-level business data (metrics, usage, revenue, incidents,
   OKRs, customer asks) to size the impact. Cite it.
4. Score each option from 1 to 5 on:
   - product impact
   - strategic alignment
   - management affinity
   - technical affinity
   - space: central, growing, depended on
   - compounding: leaves an owned asset
   - cost of delay

   Give man-months and success rate as ranges, each with its basis, and mark
   confidence as evidence or hints. Add reversibility, visibility, risk,
   dependencies or time to first signal when they could change the rank.
5. Rank by space and impact, weighted by success rate. Man-months size the
   first step, not the target. Prefer climbing: task → reusable piece → owned
   component → owned platform. Ownership comes from claiming it, not from
   finishing the stage before.
6. Act by quadrant:
   - do important and urgent work now
   - schedule and protect important work that isn't urgent
   - delegate or shrink urgent work that isn't important
   - drop the rest

   Important means high space, impact or alignment. Urgent means an evidenced
   deadline or cost of delay.
7. Name anything that overrides the ranking: a deadline, a growing risk, a
   blocker or a closing window. Only evidence can override.
8. Ask at most one question, and only if the answer could change the Pick.

## Guards

- **Someone else decides:**
  - ask in exact words, naming the decider, a date and the channel, with a
    written recap the same day
  - ask for the real goal, with a horizon. A safer stand-in doesn't count.
  - if the decision is contested and you have proof, the ask is a demo to
    every decider at once, with the decision on the agenda. It is not a
    fallback.
  - after a win, ask for the next stage within two weeks
- **An artifact:** set a send-by date. If the full version isn't ready, send a
  smaller one that day.
- **A remark, rumor or mood:** it can raise cost of delay, and nothing else.
  It never dominates.
- **Invisible value:** send the decider a short note, or rank the work as
  terminal.
- **A fallback:** give it a dated touch and an evidence-based trigger. Until
  the trigger fires, it gets touches, not pursuit.
- **Always:** set a check date, with a signal and a named fallback.

## Output

Under 300 words plus the matrix:
- stakeholders, one line each, with the source
- the impact source
- the matrix, with scores and quadrant
- any override
- the Pick, with its immediate and 1–2 year outcomes
- the next step, one line per applicable guard

Use ranges, not false precision.
