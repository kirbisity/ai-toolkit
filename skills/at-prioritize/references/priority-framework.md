# Priority framework (defaults)

The user can override any default ("speed matters more than compounding this
quarter"). State any override you apply in one line.

## Dimensions (High / Medium / Low)

| Dimension | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value now** | Solves a problem someone feels today: users, a decider or a deadline | Useful, but nobody is waiting on it | Nice to have |
| **Compounding (1–2 years)** | Leaves an owned, reusable asset: a platform, an interface, a decision record, a relationship, a skill that opens bigger work | Reusable, but not clearly owned or seen | Disappears once done (terminal) |
| **Cost of delay** | A window closes, the risk grows, or someone else takes it | Slower, but still available later | Same value next quarter |
| **Effort** | Weeks or more, or blocked | Days | Hours |
| **Confidence** | Based on evidence: allocations, decisions, data, written commitments | Mixed | Based on hints, guesses or one remark |

## Default order of preference

1. A step that both solves today's problem and opens bigger future work.
2. A small, early claim on owned scope, over a large, late, perfect artifact.
3. Enabling work: it makes the next three things easier.
4. Work that compounds only if seen, and only with its visibility step.
5. Terminal work. Do it when a deadline or a risk forces it, and keep it small.

## The compounding ladder

`terminal task` → `reusable piece` → `owned component` → `owned platform or
decision area`

Prefer the option that climbs a rung. Ownership isn't inherited: finishing
a stage of something doesn't make you the owner of its next stage. The claim
does.

## What dominates the scores

Any one of these overrides the matrix. Say it in one line:
- a mandatory deadline: legal, contractual or a hard date
- a production or safety risk that is growing
- a dependency that blocks others
- a closing window: an allocation about to be made, or a lane open to whoever asks first

## Evidence vs hints

| Evidence | Hint |
|---|---|
| An allocation made, a decision recorded, a written commitment, budget or headcount, a roadmap item with an owner | A remark in passing, a rumor, "might", tone, someone else's guess |

A hint can raise cost of delay. It can't set confidence to High.

## The claim step (template)

> **Claim:** "<one sentence: what I want to own or decide, and why I'm the
> right person>" to <named decider>, by <date>, in <channel>. Written
> follow-up the same day: <2–3 lines restating the ask and the next milestone>.

## Check and fallback (template)

> **Check by** <date>: <signal, e.g. a yes, an allocation, a reply>. If not:
> <named fallback, e.g. a smaller ask, a different decider, or drop it and
> move to option X>.
